# What to look for when buying Parmigiano Reggiano

1. **Check the protected name and origin:** The genuine cheese is always labelled Parmigiano Reggiano and carries the PDO (or DOP) mark. Milk and cheese must come from a fixed part of Italy (Emilia-Romagna), with local feed and under strict rules. Inside the EU, “Parmesan” on a hard Italian-style cheese usually means this same product. Outside the EU, “Parmesan” may be a different cheese; for the regulated one, look for “Parmigiano Reggiano”, PDO/DOP, and Italian origin on the label.

2. **Look for consortium certification on the rind:** authentic wheels that pass inspection carry the heat-branded consortium mark on the rind (alongside the dotted outline and identification coding). Cheese under 12 months cannot yet be certified or sold as Parmigiano Reggiano, minimum maturation is 12 months before it qualifies.

3. **Know the grading stamps if you buy a wedge with rind:** after tapping and inspection, a round mark means top grade; a square mark means second grade (Parmigiano Reggiano Mezzano) which still real Parmigiano, often used for grating; a triangle means it failed PDO grading and must be sold as non-Parmigiano cheese. Pre-grated products may not say “Mezzano”; a piece with rind is the easiest way to see what you are getting.

4. **Match age to the dish:** age changes flavour completely, about 12–18 months suits sandwiches, salads, and sauces; about 24 months for pasta; about 36 months for filled pasta and richer cooking; 48 months and older (including 60+) for small servings almost like dessert with honey, nuts, or fruit. 

5. **Notice an optional mountain mark:** Some cheese carries an extra mountain-product indication (PDO Prodotto di Montagna) for milk from higher ground in the zone. This is a different (often sweeter) profile than lowland wheels, not automatically superior.

6. **Treat organic as an add-on, not the definition of PDO:** organic Parmigiano Reggiano exists but is uncommon among producers; it adds farming rules on top of the same PDO standard. The PDO name and consortium controls still define authenticity; “organic” alone on random hard cheese is not a substitute.
