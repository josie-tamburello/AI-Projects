# What to look for when buying eggs

1. **Trust the shell stamp, not the packaging:** Class A eggs carry a code such as `3UK99999` — first digit = farming system (0 organic, 1 free range, 2 barn, 3 caged), next two letters = country, remainder = registered farm. Slogans and “farm” imagery do not override that digit.

2. **Use official guidance when needed:** rules and definitions are on GOV.UK (egg marketing standards); APHA: England 03000 200 301, Wales 0300 303 8268. Behind them sit the Egg Marketing Standards Regulations, UK/retained organic rules, the Animal Welfare Act 2006 and WOFAR, plus government poultry welfare guidance if you want the full detail.

3. **Organic (0) — strictest flock, range, and feed rules:** flock up to 3,000 hens; continuous daytime outdoor range with vegetation; at least 4 m² outdoor space per hen; range rested at least nine months between flocks; outdoor access from 12 weeks; no GM feed; antibiotics only to treat sick birds, not routinely. Look for an organic certifier (e.g. Soil Association, OF&G, Organic Food Federation) — some schemes add extra rules (e.g. Soil Association bans beak trimming).

4. **Free range (1) — outdoor access with looser caps than organic:** legal minimums require real daily outdoor access (your notes cite about eight hours with pop-holes open). RSPCA Assured is an example of a higher bar: flock up to 16,000 and range rested about two months — still a different profile from organic on flock size and rest periods.

5. **Barn (2) and caged (3) — both indoor-only:** barn hens never go outside. Caged hens (enriched cages) have about 750 cm² per bird, no equivalent flock cap to organic, and routine preventative antibiotics may be permitted — if you want to avoid that, prefer 0–1 or labels that explicitly restrict routine use.

6. **Treat extra logos as add-ons:** RSPCA Assured signals RSPCA welfare standards on top of law. British Lion is mainly salmonella / food-safety controls among participating producers — it is not a substitute for reading the 0–3 stamp for how the hen was housed.

7. **Question “pasture-raised” unless it is defined:** not a UK legal class alongside organic or free range; if a producer uses it (e.g. emphasis on grass and insects), check their definition and that the stamped digit still matches what you want to buy.
