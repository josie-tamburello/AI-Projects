# What to look for when buying pasta

1. **Check the type of pasta and flour used:** identify whether it is whole wheat, gluten-free, or standard pasta and prioritise pasta made from 100% durum wheat semolina, which is the highest quality. Be aware that lower-quality or different types (especially egg pasta) may use soft wheat flours such as bread flour, spelt, “00” flour or plain flour.

2. **Check the protein content:** pasta quality is strongly linked to protein levels in the wheat. Durum wheat can have up to 22% protein, while soft wheat is around 13%. Always choose pasta with at least 12% protein and ideally higher. As a rule, the higher the protein content, the better the quality of the flour, while lower protein suggests more processed, lower-quality wheat.

3. **Check the processing and drying method:** look for wording such as “slow dried” or lenta essiccazione. High-quality pasta is dried slowly (around 30–40 hours) at low temperatures (30–35°C), which preserves the structure and nutritional value of the semolina. Lower-quality pasta is dried quickly (as little as 4 hours) at high temperatures (40–85°C), which reduces quality. In general, the slower the drying and the lower the temperature, the better the pasta.

4. **Check the colour of the pasta:** good quality pasta should be pale, creamy, or off-white, indicating it is close to the natural semolina flour and has been gently processed. Avoid pasta with bright yellow, orange, or dusty brown tones, as this suggests high-temperature drying and lower quality. Better quality, lighter pasta also tends to hold sauce more effectively.

5. **Check “fresh” pasta carefully:** most supermarket “fresh” pasta is actually pasteurised and processed, not truly fresh. Check the shelf life: if it lasts only up to 4 days, it is likely genuinely fresh; if it lasts longer, it is more heavily processed despite appearances.

Generally trusted higher-quality brands include: Molisana, Di Martino, De Cecco, Rummo, Dalverde, Mancini, Cocco, Masciarelli, Verrigni, Afeltra, Benedetto Cavalieri, Martelli, and Felicetti.
