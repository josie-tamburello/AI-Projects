# What to look for when buying honey

1. **Check if it is raw or unpasteurised**  
   Look specifically for labels such as “raw honey” or “unpasteurised,” as most jars will not explicitly say “pasteurised.” Raw honey retains natural enzymes, pollen and beneficial compounds, whereas processed honey has been heated and stripped of these qualities.

2. **Check the origin**  
   Prioritise local honey where possible. Avoid labels such as “non-EU honey” or “blend of EU and non-EU honeys”, as these are more likely to be lower quality or adulterated. EU honey is generally subject to stricter standards.

3. **Check for organic certification (if relevant)**  
   “Organic honey” indicates the bees are kept under certified conditions and feed naturally without supplements, which can be a useful additional quality signal.

4. **Check authenticity for premium types (e.g. Manuka)**  
   Ensure it states a clear origin such as New Zealand or Australia and is not blended with other honeys.

5. **Understand the type of honey**  
   Identify whether it is monofloral (from one main flower, e.g. acacia or manuka) or multifloral (from a mix of flowers, e.g. wildflower or forest); this affects flavour and characteristics rather than quality, helping you choose based on preference.

6. **Check texture and crystallisation**  
   Raw honey may crystallise over time, becoming thicker or grainy. This is natural and a sign of minimal processing, whereas always-clear, runny honey is often more processed.


# How to use honey
If you care about quality, then do not pasteurise the honey in your own cup by adding boiling water above 40 degrees. Pateurised honey has no nutritional value and removes all trace of pollen and the beneficial enzymes that make honey what it is. The reason industrial producers pasteurise their honey is in order to delay crystallisation, which results in a more consistent and visually appealing product allowing it to stay on shelves for longer periods in a liquid form. Unpasteurised honey often crystallises over time, resulting in a thick and spreadable texture. Due to honey’s low moisture content and high acidity, bacteria and other harmful organisms cannot live or reproduce in honey.
